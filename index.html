Computational materials science / molecular simulation / soft matter

Kourosh Hasheminejad

I study materials with computer simulations, from single atoms up to whole films. In my doctoral work at Aalto University, I used all-atom molecular dynamics (MD), coarse-grained models, and dissipative particle dynamics (DPD) to design block copolymer coatings for paper, partly together with industry. Before that, I worked on polymer/graphene composites, and my first degree was in metallurgy. I enjoy teaching these methods, and I am now bringing machine learning into how I simulate.

    Research

    7 papers, 1 in review

    polymers

    Block copolymer coatings

    DPD and all-atom models of coatings that assemble on paper-like surfaces. I studied how the block ratio and the chemistry decide the order in the film, when the film cracks, and how much water gets in.

    surfaces

    Monolayers on silica

    All-atom models of alkylsilane monolayers on silica, and how water and chain length shape their structure. This is my newest paper, which is now in review.

    composites

    Polymer and graphene

    Polymer/graphene nanocomposites with treated interfaces, for better adhesion and shape memory. This was my M.Sc. work, which came after a B.Sc. in metallurgy.

      Simulation methods

      established and emerging

      Methods I use

      • All-atom molecular dynamics

        GROMACS and LAMMPS, for polymers, monolayers, and interfaces, dry and in water.

      • Coarse-grained MD

        Several atoms per bead, for assemblies that all-atom runs cannot reach.

      • Dissipative particle dynamics

        Soft beads for films and phase separation at the mesoscale.

      • Quantum chemistry for force fields

        ORCA scans to fit and check force field terms, such as those at the Si-C bond of alkylsilanes.

      • Analysis of structure and dynamics

        Order parameters, density profiles, hydrogen bonds, and the water next to a surface.

      Emerging methods

      • Machine learning potentials

        MACE trained on quantum chemistry data and tested in MD.

      • Active learning

        A potential that picks its own DFT calculations while it runs MD.

      • Machine-learned coarse-graining

        Bead mappings and coarse-grained force fields learned from all-atom forces.

      I am adding these to my work one project at a time. The ideas behind them are explained on the machine learning in MD page.

      Teaching

      courses and explainers

      I have developed course material and worked as a teaching assistant for Soft Materials Modelling and Computational Chemistry I at Aalto. That course material is not on this site. I have also advised five B.Sc. theses. In 2022, I founded the Modeller's Council, a biweekly forum where modelers share software demos, papers, and simulation problems, and I led it until 2025.

      The learn pages are my explainers on MD, statistical mechanics, analysis, coarse-graining, DPD, enhanced sampling, and more. Each page has live examples, such as this comparison of Newton's equations with four thermostats.
