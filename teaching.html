Teaching

Teaching molecular simulation

I enjoy explaining how simulations work, from the equations of motion to the numbers that end up in a paper. At Aalto University, I developed course material, taught exercise sessions, supervised students, and led a forum for modelers. I also write explainers with small simulations that run in the browser, so that students can change a method and see what happens. Bead Lab does the same on an iPhone, with eight simulations.

Notebooks

run on Colab

  • Dissipative particle dynamics from scratch

    Three NumPy notebooks that follow the methods chapter of my thesis. The first builds a DPD fluid from its three pair forces, the second measures the link between the Flory-Huggins χ and aij, and the third lets a block copolymer coating assemble into a layered film. Each notebook ends with exercises and worked solutions.

    dpd-teaching · not pushed yet

  • Thirteen small simulations

    Lennard-Jones, Ising, Langevin, polymers, DLA, Turing patterns, umbrella sampling, lattice Boltzmann, and active matter, followed by examples in LAMMPS and GROMACS. Each simulation is its own small repository with one notebook, checked against theory or experiment. They are good starting points for exercises.

An example

a common mistake

Students often fit the diffusion coefficient over the whole MSD curve. In this example, they can see what goes wrong. If you move the fit window into the ballistic part, or use only a few particles, the answer moves away from the true value. There are more examples like this on the diffusion page.
