Dissipative particle dynamics

DPD is the method behind most of my thesis work on block copolymer coatings. It is coarse-grained MD with very soft beads and a thermostat that conserves momentum. Because of this, it reaches the length and time scales where polymers self-assemble, while it still keeps hydrodynamics.

    Three pairwise forces

    Every pair of beads closer than the cutoff rc feels three forces along the line between them.

    • The conservative force is a soft repulsion, aij(1 − r/rc), and the size of aij sets the chemistry.
    • The dissipative force is a friction on the relative velocity, −γ wD(r)(e·vij).
    • The random force is a kick, σ wR(r) ξij / √dt.
    wD = wR2, σ2 = 2γkT (fluctuation-dissipation)

    Because the energy stays finite even when beads overlap, DPD can use a time step around 0.03 to 0.05 in reduced units, far larger than with LJ.

    What one bead means

    In DPD, a water bead usually contains Nm water molecules. With the standard density of ρ = 3 beads per rc3, this sets the real cutoff length, and matching the compressibility of water sets the repulsion between like beads.

    rc = 3.107 (ρ Nm)1/3 Å
    aii ≈ (16 Nm − 1) / (0.2 ρ) kT (a = 25 for Nm = 1)

    Try: three water molecules per bead give an rc close to 6.5 Å, which is a common choice.

    From χ to aij

    Groot and Warren showed that the excess repulsion between unlike beads maps onto the Flory-Huggins χ parameter. This is how chemistry enters a DPD model. The χ parameter can come from experiments, solubility parameters, or all-atom simulations, and project 13 learns it from MD.

    aij = aii + 3.27 χij at ρ = 3

    Try: below a χ of about 2, the single beads stay mixed, and above it, they demix. For polymers, the threshold is χN, so long chains separate at a much smaller χ.

    Why momentum conservation matters

    The DPD friction acts between pairs, so momentum is exchanged but never lost. A flow decays slowly through viscosity, as in a real liquid. In contrast, a Langevin thermostat drags every bead against a fixed background, so flows die almost at once, and hydrodynamics is lost.

    For self-assembly and phase separation, this changes the speed and even the path of structure formation.

    Try: compare the decay of the flow and the total momentum with DPD and with Langevin.

    Block copolymers form domains

    If you bond A and B beads into chains, the system cannot separate fully. Instead, it forms stripes (lamellae), cylinders, or spheres, depending on the block ratio and on χN. This is called microphase separation.

    Try: change the number of A beads per chain to make the blocks unequal.

    A coating that assembles itself

    This is the system from my thesis, simplified to 2D. Amphiphilic di-block copolymers in water sit above a hydrophilic surface, such as cellulose or starch. As the water evaporates, the hydrophilic blocks attach to the substrate, and the hydrophobic blocks form a layer above them.

    In 3D and with more realistic parameters, this process decides whether the coating is continuous or full of defects, and therefore how well it blocks water.

    Try: change the hydrophobic block length, and stop the evaporation to compare a wet and a dry film.
